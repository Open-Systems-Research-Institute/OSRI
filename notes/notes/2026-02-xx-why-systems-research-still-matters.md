# Why Systems Research Still Matters

Modern computing systems are built on layers of abstractions that are rarely
questioned by most developers. Operating systems, kernels, and low-level system
interfaces are often treated as fixed foundations rather than active areas of
design.

As software systems grow more complex and security concerns increase, many
problems are addressed through patches, frameworks, and tooling added on top of
existing systems. While this approach is practical, it can obscure deeper
questions about whether the underlying system models are still appropriate.

Systems research matters because it examines these foundational assumptions.
Choices made at the operating system and architecture level influence
performance, security, reliability, and the way developers reason about
software.

My interest in systems research comes from curiosity about these foundations:
why they are designed the way they are, what tradeoffs they encode, and what
alternative designs might look like if approached today. The goal of OSRI is not
to replace existing systems, but to study, document, and experiment with ideas
that are often considered out of scope in production environments.

This note serves as an early articulation of that motivation and will evolve as
the research develops.
